# AGL and Container-Transport Discovery Toolkit

**Purpose:** Preserve authorized operational knowledge without copying confidential AGL information or presenting informal interest as partnership evidence.

## Evidence labels

- **Observed:** Directly witnessed by the founder in normal work.
- **Reported:** Stated by an interview participant.
- **Assumed:** Working hypothesis requiring validation.
- **Approved:** Formally agreed by an authorized decision-maker.

Every note must include an evidence label. Names and contact details should be stored only with permission and outside this repository.

## Known context to validate

| Statement | Current evidence | Validation needed |
|---|---|---|
| Individual providers sometimes lack sufficient suitable trailer capacity | Observed | Quantify frequency and operational impact |
| Providers coordinate/subcontract capacity informally | Observed | Map approval, pricing, and responsibility process |
| Some AGL drivers consider AFOS useful | Reported informally | Structured task-based driver interviews |
| A shared capacity-coordination workflow may improve fulfilment | Assumed | Real requests and completed pilot movements |
| AGL will participate in a pilot | Not established | Written approval from an authorized representative |

## Current-workflow interview

1. How does a genuine container transport request first arrive?
2. What information is usually missing?
3. Who confirms the price and transport date?
4. Who checks available trucks, trailers, and drivers?
5. How is 20-foot versus 40-foot compatibility determined?
6. Who approves subcontracted capacity?
7. How does a provider accept or reject work?
8. How is the driver assigned and contacted?
9. Which movement milestones are reported today, by whom, and through what channel?
10. What counts as delivery evidence?
11. What are the most common exceptions and who resolves them?
12. Where do delays, duplicate calls, or misunderstandings occur?
13. What information must never be visible to another provider or customer?

## Driver task interview

Ask the driver to describe, then demonstrate using a paper or clickable prototype:

1. Receiving an assignment.
2. Confirming that the assignment was understood.
3. Reporting readiness and dispatch.
4. Reporting arrival at pickup and destination.
5. Reporting a breakdown or delay.
6. Uploading or identifying delivery evidence.

Record phone type, typical connectivity, language preferences, assistance required, errors, unnecessary steps, and terminology corrections.

## Provider/dispatcher interview

- What makes declared capacity genuinely usable?
- How frequently can availability change?
- Who is authorized to declare capacity and accept work?
- How much response time is realistic?
- What causes a confirmed resource to become unavailable?
- What information is needed before accepting an offer?
- Can one request be split safely across providers?
- What makes a digital workflow less practical than calls or WhatsApp?

## Demand-side interview

- What request information is known at booking time?
- How many providers are normally contacted?
- How long does confirmation normally take?
- What creates confidence that capacity is confirmed?
- Which statuses are useful versus unnecessary?
- What cancellation, delay, and delivery evidence is expected?

## Required discovery gate

Before live pilot approval, obtain evidence from at least:

- Drivers
- Provider dispatch/operations staff
- A provider decision-maker
- Freight forwarder or demand-side coordinator
- AFOS operations role

The precise participant count should prioritize repeated operational evidence over arbitrary volume.
